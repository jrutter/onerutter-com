If you're getting into Grok Bot, [Grok Market](https://grokmarket.io/categories/research) is a great place to start. It's a directory of public bot templates, and the research section alone is full of ideas: bots that collect papers and code into one reading queue, check claims against the live web with dated citations, track competitors or earnings releases, watch FAA notices before a launch, or send a focused morning briefing on local housing agendas.

There are even bots about bots. One asks you a few questions and then designs a focused Grok Bot for one clear job. Another reviews a public template, flags risks, and tells you whether to install it or rebuild it yourself.

What I'm still curious about is what's behind each one. A template is a shareable copy of someone's bot setup: what it's for, how it should behave, the step-by-step skills it follows, and the schedules it runs on. I'd love to see more of those instructions out in the open, because that's where the real craft is. I want to know how people break a job down, where they add checks, and when the bot stays quiet.

I'll be digging into a few of these and probably sharing my own. Watch this space.
